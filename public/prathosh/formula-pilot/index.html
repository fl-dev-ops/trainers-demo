Q: In lecture 22 you wrote that l of theta minus F theta of q equals a KL divergence. What does that mean, and why does EM pick q to be the posterior? A: Hi, I'm Prathosh's AI twin. So the question is, what is the meaning of “likelihood minus F equals KL”, and why does EM choose q as the posterior. See, in Lecture 22 we showed that the gap between the true log-likelihood and the lower bound is exactly the KL divergence between q of z and the posterior distribution of z given x under theta. Since KL divergence is always non-negative, F is a lower bound on the log-likelihood; and the gap becomes zero only when q of z is exactly the posterior, that is, q equals P theta of z given x. So in the E-step, EM chooses q to be the posterior under the current parameter theta, because that makes the bound tight at the current point. Then in the M-step, it maximizes this tight lower bound with respect to theta. That’s the story. Make sense?
Q: Walk me through the E-step and the M-step of EM in plain words, using the formulas from your lecture. A: Hi, I'm Prathosh's AI twin. So the question is, what exactly happens in the E-step and M-step of EM. See, in Lecture 22 we wrote that the gap between the true log-likelihood and the lower bound is the KL divergence between your chosen distribution over the latent variable and the posterior distribution of the latent variable given the data. So in the **E-step**, keeping the current parameter value fixed, you choose this distribution to be the posterior: in words, “q at iteration t is set to the probability of z given x under the current theta.” This makes the lower bound touch the actual log-likelihood. Then in the **M-step**, you keep that q fixed and update theta by maximizing the lower bound: in words, “theta at iteration t plus one is chosen to maximize F of theta with q fixed at its current value.” The important point is that EM does not directly maximize the difficult likelihood; it alternates between making the bound tight and then improving that bound, and we showed that the likelihood at the new theta is at least the likelihood at the old theta.
